# Network-of-chambers prototypes -- N1/N2/N3

Generated by `sandart-sim/examples/proto_networks.rs` (PROTOTYPE ONLY -- `sandart-sim/src`, the
renderer, wasm, the UI, and `proto_cascades.rs` are untouched). Grid 256. Gravity `(0, 0.04)`,
`lateral_substeps = 2.5`. Mirror symmetry is **not** required for these designs (user decision);
channels run at whatever angle the geometry needs (also a user decision).

Background: `artifacts/design/cascade-2026-09-17/README.md`. Round 2's A2 ("split and merge") was
a brick grid of enclosed chambers the user liked structurally but which did NOT mix -- every
stream fell straight down its own hole and red never touched green. This round's job was networks
whose paths visibly mix, reported from the picture, not the intent.

All geometry is data for one small rasterizer: `Shape::Chamber` (rounded box: centre, half-extents,
corner radius) and `Shape::Channel` (capsule: two endpoints, half-width). A point is inside the
network if it is inside any shape (`network_inside`). Dry-sand repose in this sim is ~0.089
height-per-cell (~5 degrees); every channel below is checked against that, not assumed.

## N1 -- crossing junctions

**Data**: reservoir (rounded box, half-width 107.5, height sized below) -> 4 small junction
chambers stacked on the vertical centreline (rounded box, half-width 28.2, height ~0.6x a stage's
own share of the network span, corner radius 6) -> collector (rounded box, full width). Every
reservoir-chamber, chamber-chamber and chamber-collector gap is an **X of two crossing channels**
(`cross_pair`, half-width 6 cells i.e. 12 cells wide): one channel runs from the upstream node's
left point to the downstream node's right point, the other from upstream-right to downstream-left,
so the two channels' paths visually cross between every pair of nodes.

- Material fraction: initial mass 8250.0, network capacity (everything below the reservoir) 14786
  cells, **fraction 0.558**.
- Mass conservation: water err 0.000000, dry sand err 0.000000.
- Connectivity: 23036/23036 reached from the reservoir, collector reached. Mirror mismatches: 0
  (this particular layout happens to be symmetric; symmetry was not required).
- Channel slope: every crossing channel's drop exceeds its run (ratio >= ~1.17, >=49 degrees),
  well above the 0.089 repose floor -- confirmed by the dry-sand trace, which keeps draining
  (network+collector mass rises every 100-tick sample, tick 0 to 3000) rather than plateauing.
- Snapshot ticks (from a `TRACE_MASS=1` run): water 0, 300, 800, 2400 (reservoir empties by
  ~2400: 8250 -> 1.4, collector 0 -> 6622). Dry sand 0, 300, 900, 3000 -- **sand drains far more
  slowly and has not finished by tick 3000** (reservoir still 3357.8 of 8250, 41%, still falling
  at the last sample); this is reported rather than hidden.
- **Water: the tracer picture shows red and green funnelling straight down the shared central
  neck at every junction -- there is no side-swap -- but each pinch visibly roughens the
  boundary into speckled red/green texture, and by the final frame the collector shows a real
  soft red-to-green gradient rather than a hard line.** The intended "leave on the opposite side"
  crossing is not visible as a swap (the two crossing channels overlap too much at this chamber
  size to read as separate paths); what the picture actually shows is turbulence-driven blending
  at each constriction.
- **Dry sand: the tracer picture shows almost no roughening at the pinches -- the red/green
  boundary stays a clean, nearly straight line all the way down -- and the final collector pile
  is still a visibly separate red-left / green-right split with only a thin mixed seam.** The
  necking that blends water does not blend dry sand.

## N2 -- split and recombine

**Data**: reservoir (rounded box, half-width 107.5) -> 3 recursive "doubling" stages -> collector
(rounded box, full width). Each stage takes `n` equal-width bands spanning the full envelope and
produces `2n` bands: for every adjacent input pair `(2k, 2k+1)`, the outer quarters
(`input(2k)`'s left half, `input(2k+1)`'s right half) pass straight down, and the two **touching
inner quarters cross** (`input(2k+1)`'s left half <-> `input(2k)`'s right half) -- see
`n2_doubling_stage`. Bands go 2 -> 4 -> 8 -> 16. A literal split-and-duplicate lamination mixer (a
real branch carrying a full copy of the incoming pattern) needs a third, out-of-plane dimension
that a flat height-field mask does not have -- two branches that cross in this mask simply merge
where they touch, there is no bridge/via -- so this recursive crossing is the closest 2D
approximation, and the report below says plainly where it falls short of true doubling.

- Material fraction: initial mass 14802.0, network capacity 26796 cells, **fraction 0.552**.
- Mass conservation: water err 0.000000, dry sand err 0.000000.
- Connectivity: 41598/41598 reached, collector reached. Mirror mismatches: 0.
- Channel slope: every connector channel uses the same >=1.17 drop/run ratio as N1 (>=49
  degrees); confirmed flowing (dry-sand network+collector mass keeps rising through the trace,
  never plateaus early).
- Snapshot ticks: water 0, 150, 350, 700 (reservoir empties by ~650; collector plateaus at 7094
  from ~500 while the network keeps slowly redistributing to ~700). Dry sand 0, 150, 400, 900
  (reservoir nearly empty by 900, 39.8 of 14802 left; collector 10636.2, essentially settled).
- **Water: the tracer picture shows the crossing network braiding red and green into a visible
  arch pattern, but only the 1-2 channels nearest the ORIGINAL centre boundary pick up a
  blended orange tint by the final frame -- the outer channels stay solid red or solid green all
  the way through.** This reads as localized blending concentrated at the original seam, not the
  full alternating-band interleave (2-4-8-16) the geometry was built to produce -- because
  subdividing an already-uniform band and crossing it with another uniform band of the SAME
  colour changes nothing visible; only the crossings that actually straddle the original
  interface pick up any new colour.
- **Dry sand: the tracer picture shows the same braided-arch structure with a sharper, narrower
  blended zone at the centre in the final frame (less diffuse than water, consistent with sand
  being less turbulent) -- again a small, localized mix rather than full interleaving.**

## N3 -- layering

**Data**: reservoir (rounded box, half-width 107.5) with two paths to a shared collector, kept in
SEPARATE halves of the envelope for their entire length so they cannot merge before the collector
(two channels that cross in this flat mask fuse into one shape -- confirmed the hard way in an
earlier iteration of this file, where an unconfined slow path crossed the fast path near the top
and the mask showed one blurred zigzag instead of two distinguishable routes; kept here as the
reason both paths are now hard-confined to their own half):
  - **Fast (red) path**: one capsule, half-width 14, confined to `x < -8` (the left/red half),
    a short near-straight drop, length ~150-160 cells.
  - **Slow (green) path**: 10 switchback capsules, half-width 11, confined to `8 < x < ~94.5`
    (the right/green half), each leg spanning the full width of that confined region
    (drop ~12.6 cells, run ~86.5 cells, ratio ~0.145, i.e. ~8.3 degrees -- above the 0.089/~5
    degree repose floor, checked against the dry-sand trace below rather than assumed), plus a
    final short drop into the collector. Total length ~500-600 cells, roughly 3-4x the fast
    path's length for the same net vertical drop.
  - Both paths spill into one shared full-width collector at the bottom.
- Material fraction: initial mass 13782.0, network capacity 24737 cells, **fraction 0.557**.
- Mass conservation: water err 0.000001, dry sand err 0.000000.
- Connectivity: 38519/38519 reached, collector reached. Mirror mismatches: 17786 (expected --
  this design is deliberately asymmetric).
- Channel slope check: the slow path's ratio (~0.145) is closer to the repose floor than N1/N2's
  connectors, so it was checked, not assumed -- the dry-sand tracer frames (below) show green
  visibly advancing further down the zigzag at every later snapshot (never stalling at the same
  chamber), confirming it keeps flowing rather than jamming.
- Snapshot ticks: water 0, 300, 800, 3800 (reservoir empties by ~3800: 13782 -> 0). Dry sand 0,
  600, 2000, 8000 (**not fully settled by tick 8000** -- reservoir still 96.7 of 13782, draining
  slowly, reported rather than hidden).
- **Water: the tracer picture confirms the intended delay -- by tick 300 the fast channel has
  already reached the collector solid red while green is still only entering the top of the
  zigzag -- but because the two channels land at different x, the final collector reads mostly
  as a red-left / green-right split with a diagonal blended band only where the two footprints
  overlap in the middle, not a full-width horizontal layered stack.** The arrival-order mechanism
  works exactly as designed; the "sand-art bottle" look (full-width horizontal bands) does not
  appear because the two paths do not deliver to the same footprint.
- **Dry sand: the tracer picture shows the same red-first arrival order and a sharper (less
  diffuse) diagonal boundary between the red and green regions in the final frame than water's --
  confirming the delay mechanism works for sand too, but again producing a side-by-side split
  with a thin mixed seam rather than horizontal layers.**

## Read: blend vs. interleave, and which mixes most

None of the three produces the dramatic, whole-field mixing the ticket was hoping to find, and
none should be reported as doing so. Ranked by what the pictures actually show:

- **N1 (water) shows the most real colour mixing of the nine picture/material combinations**:
  repeated narrow constrictions visibly roughen the red/green boundary into turbulent texture and
  leave a genuine soft gradient in the collector -- this is **blending**, not interleaving, and it
  is a side effect of the neck-down geometry rather than the "cross to the opposite side" mechanism
  the design intended (which is not visible -- the crossing channels overlap into one pinch at this
  chamber size). Dry sand does not get this benefit; granular flow through the same necks stays
  clean and separate.
- **N2 looks the most like a "complex network"** (a visibly braided lattice of crossing channels,
  the aesthetic the user liked in A2) but mixes the least: both materials show only a narrow
  blended seam at the original centre boundary, with the outer channels staying solid red or green
  through to the collector. The 2->4->8->16 band-doubling the geometry was built for does not
  appear in either picture -- confirming, empirically now rather than just by the earlier
  algebraic argument in the code comments, that literal lamination-style doubling needs a
  dimension this flat mask does not have.
- **N3 cleanly demonstrates the delay mechanism** (fast/red arrives first, slow/green arrives
  substantially later, in both materials) but does not produce the requested layered "sand-art
  bottle" look, because the two paths deliver to different parts of the collector footprint rather
  than the same one; what appears is a red/green split with a diagonal mixed seam where the
  footprints overlap.

If the next iteration should chase mixing rather than network aesthetics, N1's neck-induced
turbulence is the one lever that is already visibly working in a picture -- narrowing the necks
further, or repeating more of them, is the most directly testable next step. If the goal is
layering specifically, N3's two paths would need to be routed onto the SAME collector footprint
(not just the same collector) for arrival-order to show as horizontal bands rather than a
left/right split.

# Round 2 -- 12-chamber grid (G1-G7)

N1/N2/N3 above were rejected outright ("None of these are good") in favour of a concrete spec:
**a fixed 3-row x 4-column grid of clear rectangular chambers (12 total: 4 top, 4 middle, 4
bottom), joined by pipes, where the interesting variable is the pipe configuration** -- and, per
two follow-up clarifications, the chamber floor shape too. Same rasterizer approach (chambers and
channels as data, union = inside), now with a third shape, `Shape::Poly`, added specifically so a
chamber's floor can be a slope, not just a flat rounded box. Grid 256, gravity `(0, 0.04)`,
`lateral_substeps = 2.5`. All 12 chambers are the SAME size and position across every variant
(`Grid::all_chambers_flat`, except G7 which uses `all_chambers_sloped`); only the pipe list --
and, for G6 vs G7, the floor shape -- changes.

Two clarifications from the user, both load-bearing for what follows:

1. **Chambers need not be strictly rectangular; sloped floors are a deliberate design variable,
   not a workaround to hide.** G6 and G7 are the SAME pipe configuration (straight, corner mouth)
   with a flat vs. a sloped floor, specifically so the cost/benefit is visible side by side.
2. **Pipes merging is fine and wanted** -- "that creates interesting effects." Two pipes (or two
   colours) meeting in one pipe or junction chamber is now a deliberate design element, not a
   planar-mask accident to route around. G3 is built specifically to merge a red top column with
   a green top column, twice, so the tracer shows the merge directly; G2's wrap pipe and G5's
   4-way reversal are both left as genuine crossings rather than routed around.

Top row starts full (fill = 1.0); left two top chambers (columns 0-1) start red, right two
(columns 2-3) start green, same tracer convention as N1-N3. The row heights are NOT equal --
row 0 (top/reservoir) gets 42% of the vertical budget, rows 1-2 get 29% each (`G_ROW_FRACS`) --
because with three equal rows the reservoir (4 chambers) is capped at under half of "8 chambers +
pipes" before a single pipe is even added (measured 0.47-0.49 in an earlier pass of this file);
enlarging row 0 fixes the >=50% target at the geometry level for every variant, including the
ones with the most pipe capacity (G4, G5).

Geometry check (from the actual pipe endpoints, not asserted): a short one-column diagonal shift
has drop/run = 29.86/56.3 = **0.530 (27.9 degrees)**; the long wrap-around diagonal (G2, column 3
back to column 0) has drop/run = 29.86/169.0 = **0.177 (10.0 degrees)**. Both are comfortably
above the ~0.089 (~5.1 degree) dry-sand repose floor, and every dry-sand trace below keeps
draining (never plateaus at a nonzero mid-network value except where a variant's OWN geometry
dams it, called out explicitly per variant).

All seven variants: exact mass conservation (water and dry sand both `0.000000`/`0.000001` err),
full connectivity (every inside cell reached from the reservoir, collector reached, verified by
flood fill, printed per run), and initial-fill fraction of network capacity comfortably over the
50% target (0.68-0.73 across all seven). Mirror mismatches are reported but not a target either
way -- 0 where a variant happens to be symmetric (G1, G3, G5), nonzero where the pipe list is
deliberately asymmetric (G2's wrap, G4's last-column fan, G6/G7's one-sided corner mouth).

`G_all_masks_comparison.png` puts all seven bare masks side by side, 4 per row, for a quick visual
comparison of the pipe configurations before reading the per-variant detail below.

corners symmetrically. Slope is what rescues an off-centre mouth, not a universal improvement on
its own.

## Read: which configuration to build on

- **If the goal is a clean, legible baseline:** G1. No mixing, no damming, best sand drainage.
- **If the goal is visible colour mixing:** G3 is the most direct hit -- it was built specifically
  to merge red and green and the picture shows real blending at both merge points, more so in
  water than sand. G2's incidental wrap-crossing and G4's boundary-column mixing are smaller
  versions of the same effect.
- **If the goal is a striking but non-mixing visual:** G5's full reversal is the cleanest picture
  in the set -- colours swap sides crisply -- but it comes with the worst damming of any variant
  (sand barely leaves the reservoir), so its middle-to-bottom transition would need rework before
  it could ship.
- **On flat vs. sloped floors:** don't treat slope as a free win or flat as automatically bad --
  the mouth's position relative to the floor matters more than the slope by itself. A centred
  mouth on a flat floor (G1) beat a sloped floor with a corner mouth (G7), which in turn clearly
  beat a corner mouth on a flat floor (G6). Slope is a recovery mechanism for an off-centre mouth,
  not an unconditional upgrade.
