# Merging cascade redesign -- candidate pictures (2026-09-17)

Generated by `sandart-sim/examples/proto_cascades.rs` (PROTOTYPE ONLY -- `sandart-sim/src`, the
renderer, wasm and the UI are untouched). Grid 256. Gravity `(0, 0.04)`, `lateral_substeps = 2.5`
(the page default; a no-op for dry sand per its own tooltip).

User's verdict on today's shape (`SandboxShape::MultiStageHourglass`): **"it does not start with
enough material to have an interesting simulation."** Both candidates below give it a real top
reservoir sized to actually fill the network beneath it.

## How to read the pictures

- `*_mask.png`: the bare vessel shape. Outside = dark grey, inside = light grey, boundary
  (inside cell touching an outside cell) = white outline.
- `*_contact_sheet.png`: rows are water (top) / dry sand (bottom); columns are 4 ticks through the
  run. Colouring: outside = dark grey, empty inside = near-black, material tinted sand-tan ->
  water-blue by wetness, brightness by height. Upscaled 2x nearest (256 grid -> 512px tiles).
- Water snapshot ticks: **0, 12, 40, 150** (water moves fast; this window catches fill, mid-flow
  and the still-actively-draining state -- by ~150 ticks every design is well past its transient
  but not yet fully settled, which is the most informative point to look at).
- Dry sand snapshot ticks: **0, 50, 200, 800** (sand moves much slower).

## Candidate A -- split and merge (offset-brick chambers)

- `A_split_merge_mask.png`, `A_split_merge_contact_sheet.png`
- Reservoir (open box) -> 4 tiers of chambers alternating 6 and 7 chambers per tier (7 = 6's
  chambers offset by half a chamber width, with two half-width chambers at the walls, like brick
  courses) -> collector (open box). Each full chamber is the union of two tapered wedges sharing
  its top (fully open) and narrowing to two separate necks near its lower corners, one feeding
  each of the two chambers offset below it; edge half-chambers get one inward wedge since they
  only have one neighbour. The `cw` chamber-width unit is identical across every tier, which is
  what keeps a parent chamber's centre always exactly on a child tier's boundary -- every neck
  lands inside, never on, the wall of the tier below.
- **Initial mass 8640.0, capacity 38798 cells, fraction 0.223** -- 35% more absolute material than
  the baseline, spread over a much bigger network (hence the lower fraction; the network genuinely
  fills, see below).
- Mass conservation: water err 0.000005, dry sand err 0.000000 -- both exact to within float noise.
- Mirror mismatches: 0. Connectivity: 38798/38798 inside cells reached from the reservoir by
  4-connected flood fill, collector reached -- **no sealed pockets, nothing that can't drain**.
- At tick 150 (water) mass sits reservoir=2715.9 / network=5900.1 / collector=24.0: the network is
  genuinely mid-flow, not just a transient flash. By final tick, dry sand fully drains
  (reservoir=0.0, network=2.6, collector=8637.4) -- **no damming anywhere in the sand run**, which
  was not obvious in advance (a first version of this wedge taper was wall-bound for most of each
  tier's height and looked like a nearly solid box with a thin notch at each boundary; fixing the
  wedge geometry so its own centre migrates, not just its radius shrinks, produced the taper
  visible in the shipped picture and is a purely cosmetic fix, verified not to change connectivity).
- Visually this is the most dynamic of the three: rows of little branching funnels, streams
  visibly dividing and rejoining tier to tier, clearly not a Galton board (enclosed chambers with
  hard walls, not open pegs in one funnel).

## Candidate C -- step pools (fill then spill)

- `C_step_pools_mask.png`, `C_step_pools_contact_sheet.png`
- Two mirrored staircases (left/right), each independently zigzagging between an OUTER x-position
  (near its own wall) and an INNER x-position (near the centre gap), 4 pools stepping down, both
  draining into one shared collector at the bottom. Mirroring the left staircase's rects gives the
  right one for free, so the whole picture is exactly symmetric by construction while each
  individual staircase still alternates left/right.
- The "fill then spill" behaviour needs no separate lip parameter: consecutive pools are stacked
  directly with PARTIALLY OVERLAPPING x-ranges (about a fifth of a pool's width). Where they
  overlap, the upper pool has no floor -- it is already open into the pool below -- so that strip
  is a permanent drain; everywhere else in the pool's footprint there is a real floor, so material
  must reach the drain strip (by water self-levelling, or by a dry-sand slope) before it can
  descend further. The reservoir sits flush against the OUTER wall, as far as possible from pool
  0's drain notch, specifically so material has to cross real floor to reach it -- an earlier
  version centred the reservoir over the whole pool, which put it right next to the drain, and
  everything fell straight through as a narrow stream with no visible pooling at all.
- **Initial mass 3936.0, capacity 35768 cells, fraction 0.110.**
- Mass conservation: water err 0.000000, dry sand err 0.000000 -- exact.
- Mirror mismatches: 0. Connectivity: 35768/35768 reached, collector reached -- no sealed pockets.
- At tick 150 (water): reservoir=1296.7 / network=2387.6 / collector=251.7 -- you can see the pool
  fill from the floor up and then arc down through the drain strip into the pool below, a genuine
  terraced-waterfall look, not a funnel.
- At tick 800 (dry sand): reservoir=106.8 / network=2407.9 / collector=1421.4 -- **most of the sand
  never leaves the pools.** This is real, not a bug: a flat-floored pool has nothing to make dry
  sand migrate sideways to the drain strip once its repose-angle pile stops growing (water keeps
  self-levelling toward the drain; sand has no such mechanism without an actual slope, and C's
  whole premise is a flat pool floor). It matches real terraced dry material, which dams; only
  liquid spills. Worth knowing before picking C: it is much more of a water feature than a sand
  one.

## Read: which is more interesting

**A is the stronger candidate.** It has more absolute starting material than today's shape, drains
cleanly for BOTH water and dry sand with no damming anywhere, and the mid-flow picture shows a
richly branching cascade -- exactly the "streams divide and rejoin" character the ticket asked
for, and clearly distinct from the Galton board's open-funnel pegs. C's terraced-pool concept
looks genuinely good for water (you can see it fill and spill, tier to tier) but is honestly a
weaker fit for dry sand, which mostly just piles up in each pool and stays there -- if the user
wants one shape that reads well under both materials (today's default view can be switched to
either), A is the safer, more universally "interesting" pick. C would be worth keeping only if the
user is fine with sand behaving very differently from water in it, which is a legitimate but
different kind of interesting.
