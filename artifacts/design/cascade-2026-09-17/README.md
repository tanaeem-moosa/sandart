# Merging cascade redesign -- candidate pictures

Generated by `sandart-sim/examples/proto_cascades.rs` (PROTOTYPE ONLY -- `sandart-sim/src`, the
renderer, wasm and the UI are untouched). Grid 256. Gravity `(0, 0.04)`, `lateral_substeps = 2.5`.

User's verdict on today's shape (`SandboxShape::MultiStageHourglass`): **"it does not start with
enough material to have an interesting simulation."**

## Round 1 -- what the pictures actually showed (kept as `r1_*` files)

Round 1 shipped candidates A (split/merge) and C (step pools) with a report that did not match
their own pictures. Corrected account, from the pictures themselves, not from what the geometry
was meant to do:

- **`r1_A_split_merge_*`**: renders as a regular lattice of triangular teeth. Water and sand both
  fall as ~13 straight PARALLEL COLUMNS and end as a flat layer at the bottom. Nothing visibly
  divides or rejoins -- it reads as a bed of nails, closer to a Galton board than intended.
- **`r1_C_step_pools_*`**: the material BYPASSES the pools. In both water and sand, two streams
  fall straight down the sides from the corner reservoirs to the collector; no pool visibly fills
  or spills.
- Material: A started at 22% of its own network's capacity and C at 11%, both BELOW today's
  shape's 26% -- neither actually answered "not enough material."

The root causes, found only by looking at the pictures:
- A's per-chamber taper used a wedge whose radius at the top already exceeded the distance to the
  chamber's own wall, so the wall (not the wedge) was the binding constraint for most of the
  tier's height -- visually a flat box with a thin notch only right at the tier boundary.
- C's floor-gap "drain" existed for a pool's ENTIRE height, not just near the top, so it was a
  permanent side leak, not a weir -- material never had to fill anything before draining through.

## Round 2 -- B0 (control), A2, C2

Fixes: **B0** is a control -- today's shape, geometry completely unchanged, with only the initial
fill enlarged. **A2** replaces the taper with literal enclosed "closed bowl" chambers (flat sides,
two small FIXED-size holes near the floor, not a funnel) with a real wall between neighbours at
every height. **C2** replaces the floor-gap with a genuine weir: the only opening in a pool's
downstream wall is a short slit near the TOP, connected through a chute that is physically
separated (a real wall gap) from the pool for the rest of its height, so nothing can leave a pool
below the weir line.

A colour tracer (the reservoir's left half red, right half green at t=0; colours advect with the
material -- `sandart_sim::pack_rgba`/`color_channel`, no engine changes) makes dividing, rejoining
and fill order checkable from the picture rather than asserted. One extra `*_tracer_sheet.png` per
design.

Water snapshot ticks were chosen from measured fill/spill times (`TRACE_MASS=1 cargo run ...`
prints a mass-vs-tick trace every 100 ticks; see the tables below), not guessed -- round 1's
0/12/40/150 was too short to see anything settle.

### B0 -- control: today's shape, unchanged geometry, bigger reservoir

- `B0_control_mask.png`, `B0_control_contact_sheet.png`, `B0_control_tracer_sheet.png`
- The vessel walls are bit-for-bit today's `MultiStageHourglass` (n=8). Only the fill threshold
  moved down (past tier 0, into part of tier 1) to reach the material target.
- **Initial mass 9992.0, network capacity 14920 cells (below the enlarged reservoir), fraction
  0.670** -- comfortably over the 50% target.
- Mass conservation: water err 0.000000, dry sand err 0.000000. Mirror mismatches: 0.
  Connectivity: 24912/24912 reached, collector reached.
- Water ticks **0, 300, 700, 1100** (reservoir empties and the system reaches its final
  equilibrium by ~1000-1100: reservoir 9992->0, network 0->7452, collector 0->2540). Dry sand
  ticks **0, 400, 1000, 2200** (settles by ~2200: network 7237, collector 2752, reservoir ~0).
- The tracer picture shows tier 1's chambers as the first place red and green visibly meet (their
  boundary runs straight down the middle of the picture, matching the vessel's own mirror axis),
  and by the final frame the collector shows a clean red-left / green-right split -- material from
  both halves does reach the shared collector, though this is colours sitting side by side rather
  than blending.
- Read: this is the simplest possible fix for the user's actual complaint, and it works -- more
  than 2/3 of what's below the reservoir gets filled before draining. It doesn't add any new
  visual structure, which is the honest tradeoff for touching nothing.

### A2 -- real split and merge

- `A2_split_merge_mask.png`, `A2_split_merge_contact_sheet.png`, `A2_split_merge_tracer_sheet.png`
- Reservoir (open box) -> 4 tiers of ENCLOSED chambers alternating 4 and 5 per tier (brick offset:
  5 = 4's chambers shifted half a chamber width, with 2 half-width chambers at the walls) ->
  collector. Each chamber is a real closed bowl: flat-sided box for the top ~78% of its height,
  narrowing only in the bottom ~22% to two (or, for the half-width edge chambers, one) small FIXED
  holes -- not a taper. A real wall (3 cells) separates every chamber from its neighbour at every
  height, including the box portion -- the round-1 bug (chambers abutting with no wall in the box
  region) is fixed and re-verified from the picture: `A2_split_merge_mask.png` now shows distinct
  boxes with visible dividing walls, not one continuous banded trough.
- **Initial mass 11016.0, network capacity 21864 cells, fraction 0.504.**
- Mass conservation: water err 0.000000, dry sand err 0.000000. Mirror mismatches: 0.
  Connectivity: 32880/32880 reached, collector reached -- every hole lands inside, never on the
  wall of, a chamber in the tier below.
- Water ticks **0, 150, 350, 900** (collector starts filling by tick 200 already and the system is
  fully settled by ~900: reservoir 0, network 3024, collector 7992). Dry sand ticks
  **0, 300, 700, 1800** (settles by ~1800: network ~103, collector 10911).
- **Tracer finding, honestly: mixing is barely visible.** At tier 1 (the first row whose middle
  chamber structurally receives from both a red tier-0 chamber and a green one) there is a small
  yellow-tinted patch right at the very top-centre in the water frames -- real but faint -- and it
  does not propagate: every stream below that stays purely red or purely green all the way to the
  collector. The connectivity check confirms the holes DO land in shared chambers (as designed),
  but material free-falls through in ~1 tier's height per few ticks, too fast to reside and
  redistribute sideways before it's already through the next hole. **The geometry is structurally
  doing its job (verified by connectivity), but the picture does not show robust visible mixing --
  the ticket's own bar for success. Flagging this rather than calling it a win.**

### C2 -- real step pools (weir outlet)

- `C2_step_pools_mask.png`, `C2_step_pools_contact_sheet.png`, `C2_step_pools_tracer_sheet.png`
- The reservoir is pool 0 in a single chain (reservoir -> pool 1 -> pool 2 -> pool 3 -> collector),
  alternating OUTER/INNER position and spilling right/left/right/left, mirrored for the two
  staircases. Every transition, INCLUDING reservoir -> pool 1, is a weir: a short slit near the
  TOP of the downstream wall (open only for the top 15% of that pool's own height) leading into a
  chute that is separated from the pool by a REAL wall gap for the rest of its height -- there is
  no floor drain and no side gap below the weir line anywhere in the chain.
- Sizing this needed a second fix beyond round 1: the collector's own height is
  `2*total_half - reservoir_height - 3*pool_height`, i.e. it SHRINKS as the reservoir grows
  because the whole chain shares one fixed vertical budget. Measuring the target against a
  collector sized as if the reservoir were still zero-height (what an earlier pass in this same
  round did) overshot badly and squeezed pools 2/3 and the collector down to almost nothing --
  caught from the picture (only one pool below the reservoir was visible at all) before it shipped
  here. Fixed with a small fixed-point iteration; `C2_step_pools_mask.png` now shows the full
  reservoir -> 3 pools -> collector chain with each pool's weir slit visible.
- **Initial mass 16098.0, network capacity 27261 cells, fraction 0.591.**
- Mass conservation: water err 0.000000, dry sand err 0.000000. Mirror mismatches: 0.
  Connectivity: 43714/43714 reached, collector reached.
- Water ticks **0, 120, 300, 600** (mass trace: reservoir 28218(pre-fix)/16098(post-fix) drains
  steadily while network mass climbs, reaching final equilibrium by ~500-600: reservoir 99,
  network 1951, collector 14048 -- 87% of the water has reached the collector). Dry sand ticks
  **0, 300, 700, 1500** (settles by ~1500: reservoir 50, network 21, collector 16027 -- 99.5% of
  the sand reaches the collector, faster to fully settle than water since it doesn't keep sloshing
  once it stops moving).
- **Verified from the picture: pool 1 visibly fills before pool 2 gets anything.** At tick 120 the
  reservoir has drained partway and water is pooling in the first basin, still well short of the
  next weir; by tick 300 the lower pools are visibly filling in sequence; by tick 600 the whole
  chain down to the collector shows a continuous terraced fill. The tracer confirms the two
  staircases stay independent (red stays left, green stays right) until they share the collector,
  where they sit side by side rather than mixing -- expected, since C2's two chains never
  physically join before the collector.

## Read: which is more interesting

C2 is now the strongest picture: a genuine multi-stage fill-then-spill cascade, verified stepwise
from the mass trace and the contact sheet, with both materials reaching the collector substantially
(87% water, 99.5% sand). B0 is the honest baseline fix -- it works, at the cost of adding no new
structure. A2's geometry is now correctly built (real enclosed chambers, real walls, verified
connectivity) and looks distinctly different from a Galton board, but its own stated goal --
visible colour mixing in shared chambers -- is not clearly met in the picture; call that an open
problem rather than a solved one. If the deliverable is "prove pools fill and spill," ship C2. If
it's "cheapest fix that still gives more material," ship B0. A2 needs another iteration (probably:
narrower tiers or a residence-time mechanism so streams linger long enough to redistribute) before
its mixing claim can be shown, not just structurally verified.
