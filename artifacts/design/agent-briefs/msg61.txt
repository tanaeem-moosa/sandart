Add a U-tube flow-through vessel container shape

Task #61. A U-shaped vessel rather than another funnel: a tall prefilled
reservoir arm on the left, a bottom basin connecting across, a shorter right arm
whose rim is an overflow lip, a spout off that lip, and a catch well on the far
right. Water should run down the left arm, through the basin, CLIMB the right
arm, spill over the lip and fall into the well, coming to rest with both arms
standing at the lip height.

It exists as an apparatus for the #55 hydraulic-head rewrite, and it earns that
on its first run — see below.

## Geometry

A union of five axis-aligned rects, `physics::U_TUBE_RECTS`, as fractions of
(w, h) so the shape is resolution-invariant. `neck_width` and `hourglass_curve`
are deliberately unused: there is no neck to narrow and no chamber to bend.

The strip of basin between the two arms has no rect above it, so it rasterises
as a ROOFED channel. That is the point, not an artifact: a cell under that roof
has almost no material directly overhead while being connected to an 85-row
column of water sideways of it. `column_depth`, a top-down sum of overhead mass,
says one thing there; Pascal says another. This shape puts the disagreement on
screen without any instrumentation.

The single-source-of-truth const is read by both `eval_sandbox_shape` and
`initialize_hourglass` — the latter cannot use the scalar `dy < fill_threshold`
cutoff every other shape takes, because the right arm and catch well overlap the
reservoir's dy range, so any threshold that fills the reservoir fills them too.
It fills the reservoir rect directly instead.

## What it shows today

Water, 128 grid, perfect simulation, 4000 ticks:

- The reservoir drains 17 rows down the left arm and fills the basin to the brim.
  Mass conserved to +0.000%.
- The right arm never rises. Not slowly — at all. Zero material enters it across
  4000 ticks, and zero reaches the catch well.
- It is stable and static, not oscillating or exploding.

An 85-row water column stands on a full basin whose only remaining outlet is
upward, and nothing goes up. That is #55's core defect stated as a picture: the
field has no mechanism to carry a free-surface elevation laterally, so a cell
under the roof never learns there is water above and to the left of it. It is
also why no siphon is possible. Expect this shape to look inert until the
hydraulic-head field lands, and to be the first thing worth re-running after.

## Coverage

Extending `SANDFALL_FUNNEL_SHAPES` to seven wires this shape into the existing
geometry and mass-conservation suites for free. Three new tests:

- `test_u_tube_is_one_connected_region` — flood-fill from the reservoir reaches
  every non-OUTSIDE cell, at 64/128/256/512, so a rasterisation pinch-off at one
  resolution cannot hide behind another.
- `test_u_tube_basin_has_a_roof` — the roofed strip still exists. If it ever
  vanishes the apparatus is worthless and this is the only thing that would say
  so.
- `test_u_tube_reservoir_overflows_the_lip` — pure geometry, computed from the
  rects, not hard-coded: reservoir volume above the lip (0.09) exceeds downstream
  capacity below it (0.042), so it genuinely spills rather than merely filling,
  and the catch well (0.083) cannot overflow the 0.048 that spills.

Suite: 99 passed / 1 failed / 36 ignored, the failure being the intentional
`test_water_blob_stays_left_right_symmetric_under_gravity`. Integration tests,
`sandart-render`, `sandart`, `cargo check -p sandart-wasm --target
wasm32-unknown-unknown --release` and a full `wasm-pack build` all pass.

No physics changed.
