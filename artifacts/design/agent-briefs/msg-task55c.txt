Make liquid level fast with a multigrid elliptic solve, behind a debug toggle

Task #55. Liquid did not level fast enough: a standing arch drained instead of
collapsing, and enclosed pockets in a cave took ~92 ticks to halve their level
difference. Confirmed physics rather than scheduling — ticks-to-halve was nearly
identical under the adaptive scheduler and under perfect simulation.

## The diagnosis

`ticks_to_halve` scaled almost exactly LINEARLY with grid width: 522 ticks at
w=512, 65 normalised by w/64. Linear scaling in domain width is the signature of
a CELL-RATE-LIMITED process — levelling speed was bounded by how fast
information crossed the domain at one cell per tick, not by the magnitude of the
driving potential.

That ruled out the whole class of fix aimed at the potential. A free-surface
driving head (`multiplicative_lateral_gate`, previous commits) was built, and at
w=512 it was 5% slower on arch collapse, 10% slower on pockets, bit-identical on
the draining lake, and it destroyed the angle of repose (a 19.29 degree dry sand
slope flattened to 0.41, with the non-vacuity anchor reading DrySand and Water
both at exactly 0.00). It has no yield criterion, and a granular pile at its
repose angle is a permanent surface gradient that must produce zero flow. Left
in place, gated off, as the record of a measured dead end.

## What ships here

`elliptic_liquid_level_pass`, a two-level multigrid relaxation run once per tick
before the phase loop, reading the tick's frozen `heightmap.data` and mutating
`temp_heights` so the ordinary solver starts from an already-levelled state.

The coarse level is the whole idea: the equilibrium of this diffusion problem on
a connected component is provably a single flat `eta` level, so a correction
toward it propagates globally BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by iteration count. It
is computed exactly by an O(n log n) breakpoint sweep, with connectivity from
union-find over the same edge list the fine smoother already builds — so two
wells joined through a submerged basin are treated as one component by
construction, not by special-casing. The correction is applied per-node as a
damped convex interpolation toward each cell's individually capacity-clamped
target, which is mass- and capacity-exact algebraically. The fine Gauss-Seidel
post-smoother still routes every edge through `clamp_edge_feasible`, and dropped
from 48 iterations to 8.

`eta[i] = h_ref[i] + column_depth[i] - row(i) * depth_scale`. The row subtraction
makes heads comparable ACROSS rows rather than only within one, which is what
lets a solve connect two wells through a basin at a different row than either;
`test_elliptic_eta_is_row_independent` guards it.

LIQUID-ONLY, by domain restriction rather than by a yield criterion — nodes are
interior in-mask cells with liquidity >= 0.999. This is what protects granular
material, and it is verified rather than assumed: with the pass forced on,
`test_dry_sand_has_angle_of_repose` still passes bit-identically, 19.29 degrees
-> 5.07, non-vacuity anchor DrySand 3.73 against Water 0.00.

## Measured

Propagation limit removed — the normalised figure now FALLS as the domain grows,
which flat smoothing never did:

    w=128  normalised ticks_to_halve:  64.0 off -> 21.5 on
    w=512  normalised ticks_to_halve: 65.25 off -> 9.875 on

Pocket equalisation at w=512: 29 ticks_to_halve off -> 6 on.
Arch collapse at w=512: 522 -> 464, with the best peak and ~4x less unsupported
material remaining at tick 600 than baseline.

Cost: 16.4x ms/tick at w=512 (3.4367 -> 56.27), down from 45x for the flat
48-iteration version. That is roughly 18fps — sluggish but usable, which is why
this ships as a default-off toggle rather than as the default. An earlier
scoping pass that skipped liquid-free work is included and is exact, but did not
move this number: the cost is the solve itself, not setup overhead.

## Two bugs found on the way, worth recording

1. `mult_lateral_conveyance`'s output is unbounded (~h^1.5) and was written to
   feed `flux_edge_candidate`'s momentum pipeline, not to be a bare multiplier on
   a direct mass transfer. Used raw, the residual went 0.24 -> 10.9 -> stuck at
   12.8. Fixed with a saturating weight so a per-edge step can never exceed a
   damped step toward equalising that edge.
   `test_elliptic_residual_falls_monotonically` guards it.
2. A pass that mutates `temp_heights` is silently reverted next tick unless it
   wakes the blocks it touched — the tick-end copy-back only writes blocks
   already marked `modified`. Symptom was `ticks_to_halve=None` under the
   adaptive scheduler against 12 under perfect simulation on the same scenario.

## The toggle

"Fast liquid levelling (slow)" in the Debug group, default OFF, plumbed exactly
like `fresh_pressure_field`: a plain field on `DrawingSimulation`, a plain field
write in the wasm binding with no reset, safe to re-apply on every
`syncSettings()` (which pushes the whole panel on every control change), and
carried through `set_grid_size`'s rebuild so it survives a resolution change.

With it off the simulation is bit-identical to before.

Suite: 96 passed / 1 failed / 36 ignored, the failure being the intentional
`test_water_blob_stays_left_right_symmetric_under_gravity`. Integration tests,
`sandart-render`, `cargo check -p sandart-wasm --target wasm32-unknown-unknown`,
and a full `wasm-pack build` of the deployed artifact all pass.
