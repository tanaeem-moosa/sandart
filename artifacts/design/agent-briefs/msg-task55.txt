Add task #55 levelling diagnostics and a gated multiplicative lateral head

Checkpoint commit. The gate is OFF by default and the suite is bit-identical
with it off, so this changes no shipped behaviour. It is committed to protect
the measuring instrument, which everything after this gets judged against.

## The diagnostics

`diag_task55_arch_collapse_rate`, `diag_task55_pocket_equalisation`,
`diag_task55_draining_lake_flatness` measure how fast material levels, each as a
per-tick time series with a `ticks_to_halve`, run under both the adaptive
scheduler and perfect simulation.

They establish that task #47's scheduling fix explains none of the slow
levelling. Ticks-to-halve, adaptive vs perfect: arch 71 vs 65, pockets 92 vs 92,
draining lake peak spread 15.0 vs 14.0. These are physics-level defects.

The arch metric is deliberately position-independent — material still suspended
at least a fixed physical height above the floor-connected pile's own top,
wherever it has moved to. A first version measured "did material leave its
starting rows" and reported a meaningless 1 tick, because a fully unsupported
slab free-falls intact.

`diag_task55_pocket_equalisation` uses the procedural cave shape and a genuine
communicating-vessels topology: two wells sharing a submerged basin, filled to
different levels.

## The gated multiplicative head

`multiplicative_lateral_gate` replaces the additive driving head

    driving = (h_a - h_b) + k * LATERAL_PRESSURE_SCALE * (depth_a - depth_b)

with a free-surface form, `flux ~ conveyance(depth) * grad(eta)`. The additive
form loses all lateral driving exactly where overburden correctly goes to zero,
which is the thin moving surface layer -- the one place levelling must happen.

`eta` uses raw height (geometry: how tall is the pile). `conveyance` uses
`janssen_effective_depth` and the earth-pressure coefficient K (how much of that
depth carries lateral flow), and folds in bare `h` so a shallow puddle with zero
overburden still has nonzero conveyance -- without that, every surface cell of
every pile would have zero conveyance and all surface levelling would die
silently. `column_depth` is read once per endpoint, into conveyance only, so the
Janssen saturation is not applied twice.

Conveyance exponent 1.5, the broad-crested weir form, chosen over Manning's 5/3
because this solver has no hydraulic-radius or wetted-perimeter geometry to
justify Manning's extra 1/6 power. Unswept first choice, as is
`MULT_LATERAL_SCALE = 1.0`.

## The unit bug, found and fixed

`eta = h + column_depth` mixed two unit systems. `column_depth` is pre-scaled by
`depth_scale = REFERENCE_GRID_HEIGHT / w` to be resolution-independent; `h` is a
fraction of one local cell's capacity and is never scaled. Identical physical
geometry measured at two widths:

    w= 64  depth_scale=8.0  column_depth=40.0  eta=40.4  h is 0.99% of eta
    w=128  depth_scale=4.0  column_depth=20.0  eta=20.4  h is 1.96% of eta

`eta` differed 2x by grid width alone, and the free-surface term contributed
under 2%, so `grad(eta)` was measuring a nearly pure `column_depth` gradient
rather than a surface gradient. Invisible at production w=512 where
`depth_scale == 1`; every task-55 diagnostic runs at 64 or 128.

Fixed by lifting `h` into reference-row units (`h * depth_scale`) in both `eta`
and `conveyance`. That direction and not the other: `column_depth / depth_scale`
is a local row count that scales linearly with `w`, so conveyance would scale as
`w^1.5` and `MULT_LATERAL_SCALE` would need re-deriving per resolution. Capacity
needs no correction -- it depends on wetness, never on `w`, so it cannot
introduce a resolution-dependent error.

`diag_task55_eta_depth_scale_consistency` proves the result: the driving term now
deviates 0.00% across w = 64/128/256/512 on fixed physical geometry, against up
to 2.12% before.

`test_mult_lateral_flat_surface_same_eta_different_depth_no_flux` had encoded the
old formula's flat-eta condition in its construction; under the corrected formula
that construction is no longer flat. Its construction was updated to solve the
corrected condition. The assertion -- zero lateral drift across a flat surface --
is unchanged.

## Where this leaves the approach: unproven, not validated

At production w=512 the multiplicative form gives 17-24% fewer voids on
`diag_mult_lateral_void_count_gate_on_vs_off` (19290/16701/30.39M against
additive's 23202/20791/37.78M). This is the first measurement at the resolution
where the unit bug was always a no-op.

At w=64/128 it is mixed: pocket equalisation improves 92 -> 10 ticks_to_halve,
but arch collapse slips 71 -> 73 and the w=64 void count is clearly worse.

The three levelling diagnostics hardcode hand-built pixel coordinates and have
never run at w=512. So there is production-resolution evidence for the void
proxy only, and low-resolution evidence for levelling, which is the actual
target. Closing that gap needs the diagnostics made resolution-parametric, with
care: the arch metric's "3 rows" is a fixed cell count meaning a different
physical height at each width, the procedural cave's topology is not guaranteed
to survive a resolution change, and `ticks_to_halve` counts ticks.

The known weakness in the form itself: `conveyance ~ depth^1.5` suppresses
shallow material super-linearly (depth 0.1 gives a 3x penalty against linear,
0.01 a 10x penalty), and thin films dominate the settling endgame. Measured
mean conveyance falls from 158 in a deep draining pool to 3.8-9.9 in the endgame
window. It gets slow, not dead. Note also that endgame void counts are noisy for
both forms -- additive sits at a local trough exactly at tick 160 while
multiplicative reaches comparable values by 164 -- so single-tick void
comparisons are unreliable and the trajectory diagnostics should be preferred.

This is the potential half of #55. The elliptic head solve, which supplies
propagation, is not built. Neither half is expected to be sufficient alone.
