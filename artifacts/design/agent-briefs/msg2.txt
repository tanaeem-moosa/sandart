Let a free surface rise by deleting the exposed-top pin

Task #55. The head field pinned any cell whose top face was open to air at
`own_elev = z + heights * depth_scale`. That pin made it impossible for any
free surface in the domain to ever advance upward.

For a full cell `own_elev = z + depth_scale`, which is EXACTLY the `z` of
the air cell directly above it — dry cells hold `head = z`, air pressure
being zero. A Dirichlet pin is WRITTEN rather than maxed, so the surface
cell could never read a higher head from the body beneath it either. The
driving head across every water/air interface was therefore identically

    z_air - own_elev = 0

Not small, not resolution-dependent: structurally zero, everywhere, always.
Material could be pushed UP TO a free surface and never THROUGH it. No
siphon could climb and no surface could rise.

## The pin was redundant anyway

`own_elev` is already the cell's self-term in the max update, and for a
column's topmost cell it is the largest `own_elev` in that column — that
is, the free-surface elevation. So a resting column relaxes to precisely
the value the pin used to impose, with the pin gone. The pin only ever
differed from the max in TRANSIT, which is where it did the damage.

This is why all seven static specs passed either way and none caught it:
every one of them measures an EQUILIBRIUM, and at equilibrium the free
surface elevation and the body head coincide by definition. The defect
lives exactly where the specs do not look.

The `p = 0` boundary condition belongs to the ATMOSPHERE, not to the
topmost water cell, and the atmosphere already carries it — a dry cell
holds `head = z`. The interface now compares the body's head against the
air cell's own elevation, which is the physically meaningful question:
can this body lift water to that height?

The FREE-FALL pin is a different condition (support from BELOW, not
exposure above) and is untouched.

## Measured, U-tube at w=512, reservoir primed and right arm empty

    head at surface cell        -440.00  ->  -204.00   (now the body head)
    drive across air interface     0.00  ->  -236.00   (now lifts)
    right-arm rise, 400 ticks    0 cells ->  1 cell
    total_flow                      0.00 ->  7034.79

At w=64: 0 cells / 0.00 flow -> 2 cells / 792.81 flow.

The reservoir's head (-204, its own surface elevation) was already reaching
the cell one below the right arm's surface before this change — a drive of
-236 across the submerged edge. Max-propagation had delivered the siphon
head correctly all along; the surface pin was the only thing stopping it
from doing anything.

Adds `diag_task55_u_tube_rise_in_far_arm`, the diagnostic that found this.
The dynamic suite had no test that anything ever moves UPWARD — fall,
drain-dip and speed-bound all measure downward or sideways motion — which
is the other reason this survived.
