WIP: head-driven transport, blocked on field convergence

NOT FOR PUSH. Local checkpoint. The toggle in this commit PANICS when enabled —
see below. Default off, and with it off the simulation is bit-identical
(`head_field_transport_left_untouched_matches_explicitly_disabled` passes).

## What this contains

Transport driven by the hydraulic head field, as a third branch inside the
existing flux-edge solver's driving-head expression rather than a new
mass-moving pass. That structure is deliberate: the refuted
`elliptic_liquid_level_pass` made the fast global operation move MASS, and
reusing the flux solver inherits mass conservation by construction, the
donor/acceptor clamps, and the per-edge momentum damping that bounds velocity,
instead of reinventing all three.

Plus three dynamic specs encoding the defects the user photographed: falling
water must not drift sideways, a draining vessel's surface must dip toward its
outlet, and per-tick displacement must be bounded.

## THE BLOCKER

`compute_head_field` does not converge on any realistic free surface.

    w=512, draining vessel, 47315 wet cells: 8256 sweeps, residual 0.0436 vs tol 0.001
    w=128, sim scenario,     2919 wet cells: 2112 sweeps, residual 0.1015 vs tol 0.004

It asserts on convergence rather than returning a wrong field, so it panics. That
assert is correct and must stay: an unconverged head field drives mass down a
wrong gradient, which is the exact class of defect this task exists to remove.

This is NOT a w=512 scaling problem — 2919 wet cells at w=128 is a small
scenario. The variable is not size.

## Why, and it is structural

`compute_head_field`'s fast path is a union-find "coarse jump" that resolves a
connected component in a single sweep, but only when every Dirichlet pin in that
component agrees on one value. That is precisely the case whose answer is trivial
— a body at rest under one flat surface.

A vessel that is actively draining has a surface that DIPS toward its outlet,
which is the physically correct behaviour this whole task is trying to produce.
Its pins therefore disagree by construction, the shortcut never fires, and it
falls back to plain SOR, which needs O(N^2) sweeps.

So the accelerator only accelerates the case that does not need accelerating, and
every case we actually care about is the slow one. The seven static specs all
pass because each is either a single-valued-boundary body or a uniformly pinned
falling slab — the shortcut fires in all of them. They were, without anyone
noticing, a sample of exactly the easy case.

## What it needs

A real multigrid V-cycle — restrict/prolong over the wet graph — not a one-shot
component jump. The union-find connectivity is still correct and reusable as its
coarsest level.

`test_task55_dynamic_transport_spec_scoreboard` is #[ignore]d with the numbers and
the diagnosis in its doc comment, so the blocker stays discoverable and re-runs
the moment it is lifted, rather than being deleted and rediscovered later. The
lib suite is back to its known-good shape: 101 passed / 1 failed / 45 ignored,
the failure being the intentional
`test_water_blob_stays_left_right_symmetric_under_gravity`.

Do NOT unblock this by widening the convergence tolerance, lowering the sweep cap,
or letting `compute_head_field` return an unconverged field silently.
