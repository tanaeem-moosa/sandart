Make support transitive in the static head field

Task #55, step 2b. `support_fraction` (#58) looks exactly one cell down, so
support was not transitive: in a slab of water falling through air, only the
BOTTOM row read as unsupported. Every cell above it was "resting on" a cell that
was itself full of material, so it read as fully supported. The slab got pinned
correctly at its unsupported base and incorrectly at its exposed top, and the
interior relaxed between the two.

`spec_free_fall_is_pressureless_throughout` measured that at exactly 1.000 cells
of residual pressure at the slab's TOP row, at every resolution. Small, but
backwards-signed — a resting body's pressure maximum is at its base, not its
crown — and it is the mechanism behind "falling water moves sideways", so it had
to go before transport is wired up.

The fix is a bottom-up per-column pass, so that a cell resting on falling
material is itself falling:

    effective_support[i] = min(support_fraction(i), effective_support[below])

with a plain `support_fraction(i)` where below is out of mask. `support_fraction`
already returns 1.0 for off-grid-below, casing-below and degenerate-capacity, so
all three collapse into one read of it and nothing duplicates its branching. It is
read, never modified.

MIN, not a product. Every link of a resting column reads 1.0, so `min` carries
1.0 to the top, which is what `spec_uniform_head_in_resting_open_column`
requires. A product of N terms at or near 1.0 decays through a tall resting
column by nothing but repeated multiplication and would report its upper cells as
unsupported — a column standing on solid ground is not less supported the taller
it is. Commented in place so it does not get "simplified" later.

## Measured

`spec_free_fall_is_pressureless_throughout` FAIL -> PASS, residual 1.000 cells ->
exactly 0: every cell of a transitively-unsupported slab now pins directly rather
than landing under a tolerance. All six previously-passing specs still pass
against both head sources; no regressions.

Sweeps to convergence on the falling slab, the case that had no union-find
shortcut because its boundary carried two different Dirichlet values:

    w= 64   44 -> 2
    w=128  102 -> 2
    w=256  278 -> 2
    w=512  848 -> 2

Pinning the whole slab uniformly removes the two-valued boundary entirely, so the
coarse pass jumps it in one shot and the fine sweep only settles numerically. The
U-tube case was already 1 sweep and is unchanged. Correctness and cost moved the
same direction here, which is worth noting because the previous three attempts at
this task traded one against the other.

## Two limits recorded in the code, not glossed

This is a per-COLUMN propagation. It captures no LATERAL support: material on a
ledge, or held in an arch, is supported by something not directly beneath it and
this pass is blind to it. That is force-chain mechanics and belongs to the solids
half of #55.

It also deliberately duplicates something the LOD scheduler already does. That
question is different — `fresh_overburden_must_blocks` asks "might anything in
this 32x32 block move?", a boolean, `unsupported AND has_room_to_move` — and it
gets transitivity for free over TIME via `activate_neighbor_upstream`, which
wakes the block one step upstream each tick. A one-tick lag is harmless for
scheduling and fatal for a field that must be correct within the tick it is read.
Hence a separate synchronous whole-column pass rather than reuse.

Still static-only: nothing wired into `settle_tick`, `update` or any UI.

Suite: 100 passed / 1 failed / 44 ignored, the failure being the intentional
`test_water_blob_stays_left_right_symmetric_under_gravity`.
