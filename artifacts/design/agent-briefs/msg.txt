Drive liquid transport from a max-propagated hydraulic head field

Task #55. Replaces the head field's averaging update with a max, and wires
the result into the flux solver's driving head for liquid edges.

## The field: max, not averaging

The previous field settled far too slowly to be usable. At w=512 the
textbook base case — one flat pin over a free resting column — was still
47.9 reference-rows off after the spec harness's whole tick budget, and the
pressure heat-map drew it at 47.3/255 mean brightness against
column_depth's 199.9. That is the "new-field heat-map does nothing"
symptom.

The cause was the OPERATOR, not the solver. Averaging is diffusion: a
Gauss-Seidel/SOR sweep drives the field by the discrete Laplacian, whose
SETTLING time over an N-cell chain is O(N^2) sweeps — not the O(N)
wavefront-arrival time the sweep budget was sized from. Conflating those
two is what made 8 sweeps/tick look sufficient; at N=512 they differ by a
factor of 512. An earlier attempt at a union-find "coarse jump" only ever
fired when every Dirichlet pin in a component agreed, i.e. exactly the
trivial case, so it accelerated only what did not need accelerating.

But head does not obey a Laplace equation on the wet graph. At rest it is
CONSTANT through a connected body, equal to that body's free-surface
elevation — a maximum statement, not an averaging one. So the update
collapses to one rule:

    head[i] = max(own_local_hydrostatic[i], max over wet neighbours head[j])

That is Bellman-Ford rather than Jacobi, and a Gauss-Seidel-ordered max
sweep carries a value arbitrarily far ALONG the sweep direction in a single
pass (the two-pass chamfer distance transform mechanism). The cost is the
number of direction REVERSALS in a connectivity path, not its length.

No +/-1 for vertical neighbours: head carries elevation, so "rises going
down" and "falls going up" are the same statement, head[below] ==
head[above]. The per-row increment reappears only on read-back as pressure.
No omega: extrapolating past a max yields a value no neighbour holds.

The self-term is the cell's LOCAL hydrostatic head, never its own previous
value. max is monotone, so reading history would let the field only ratchet
upward and never fall when a reservoir drains. The field is therefore a
pure function of mask + heightmap + material: no hysteresis, and in
particular no dependence on material FLOW.

Dirichlet pins are still WRITTEN, not maxed — what keeps free fall
pressureless, and what leaves any gradient at all for transport to read.

MEASURED: all 7 static specs pass at w=64/128/256/512, so the static
scoreboard's full expected_passing set is green for the first time.
Convergence takes 2 sweeps for every scenario including the U-tube; 1 sweep
converges nothing (139s of cap-burning), the negative control that makes
the 2 meaningful. HEAD_FIELD_SWEEPS_PER_TICK becomes a 32-sweep safety CAP
on an early-exiting loop rather than a fixed budget. Heat-map brightness is
now 165.08/255 against column_depth's 162.36.

## Transport: two defects, both found as a frozen simulation

Unparking the dynamic scoreboard showed total_flow = 0.0000 with the
toggle on — a blob that never fell, a vessel that never drained.

1. UNITS. The driving-head sites divided the field by depth_scale but never
   multiplied by GRAVITY_HEAD_SCALE, leaving the field branch driving every
   liquid edge 25x weaker than the branch beside it. At one row of free
   fall both branches now produce the identical 25.0, which is the check
   that says the conversion is right rather than tuned.

2. THE FIELD WAS NOT TOTAL. advance_head_field wrote only WET cells, but
   settle_tick reads head_field[nb_idx] at the DRY cell a body is about to
   fall into. That read returned a stale 0.0 against a wet cell's genuine
   z ~= -1200, so head_a - head_b came out large and NEGATIVE: every
   free-fall edge was driven UPWARD and slept. Dry cells now hold z, so
   p = head - z = 0, agreeing with head_field_to_pressure by construction
   instead of by a second special case.

All six (spec, toggle) pairs in the dynamic scoreboard now pass, and it is
no longer #[ignore]d, so a regression in head-driven transport fails a
default cargo test run.

Transport remains DEFAULT OFF (head_field_transport), and with it off the
simulation is bit-identical — head_field_transport_toggle covers that.
