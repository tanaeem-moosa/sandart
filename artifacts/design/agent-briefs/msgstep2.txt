Add a static hydraulic head field that satisfies the Pascal specs

Task #55, step 2. `task55_head_field::compute_head_field` — a pure function over a
static mask + heightmap. It is not wired into the tick loop, `settle_tick`,
`update`, or any UI, and no existing physics function was modified. Step 3 is
transport; this is the field alone.

## The formulation

Hydraulic head is a Laplace problem on the wet graph with Dirichlet conditions
wherever pressure is known to be zero:

- A cell with an exposed top face has `p = 0` there.
- A cell with nothing supporting it from below is in free fall, so `p = 0` there.
- Every other wet cell relaxes toward the average of its connected wet
  neighbours.

Those first two are the same structural condition — pressure at an exposed face
is zero — which is what lets one branch cover both. They pin to different
numeric targets, though, and getting that wrong is easy: `p` is measured at a
cell's own BOTTOM face, so an exposed top pins to `z + h*depth_scale` (its own
weight still reaches its base) while an unsupported cell pins to `z` alone
(nothing below to receive anything). Pinning both to the same target leaks
exactly one cell of weight through and gives the free-fall spec a ratio of
`1/slab_cells` instead of zero.

Equilibrium on a connected body whose pins agree on one elevation is a single
uniform head equal to that elevation. Pascal's principle falls out of the
construction rather than being special-cased, which is the whole point: a siphon
needs no code.

Support comes from `support_fraction` (#58), read-only. Connectivity reuses the
existing union-find; none of the mass-moving parts of the elliptic pass come with
it. Nothing is clamped and no material moves — this is a scalar relaxation, which
is the architectural correction this task exists for.

Residuals on the three that flipped, all previously whole units of depth_scale:
Pascal 281 reference-rows -> 0; U-tube spread 136 -> 0; falling/resting pressure
ratio 1.000 -> exactly 0.

Convergence: a component with a single-valued boundary is resolved in ONE sweep by
the union-find jump, at every resolution. The two-valued case (a falling slab,
pinned differently at top and bottom) has no such shortcut and takes 44/102/278/848
sweeps at w=64/128/256/512, plateauing at the f32 noise floor. Cost at w=512,
debug build: ~11-22ms for the U-tube case, ~115-165ms worst case. Recorded as a
starting point, not optimised — step 3's problem.

## One gap found and left open, with a spec

`spec_free_fall_has_zero_pressure` reads the slab's BOTTOM cell only, and passing
it is not the same as being right. A body in free fall has no contact force
anywhere in it, so `p` must vanish at every cell.

New spec `spec_free_fall_is_pressureless_throughout` scans the whole slab.
Measured worst residual: exactly 1.000 cells of pressure, at the slab's TOP row,
at every resolution — bounded, but real, and backwards-signed (a resting column
has its maximum at the base, not the crown).

The cause is that support is not TRANSITIVE. `support_fraction` looks one cell
down, so in a falling slab only the bottom row reads as unsupported; every cell
above it is "resting on" the cell below. The slab gets pinned correctly at its
unsupported base and incorrectly at its exposed top, and the interior relaxes
between them. The fix is to propagate support UPWARD — a cell resting on falling
material is itself falling — not to widen a tolerance. Left failing and recorded
in the scoreboard rather than papered over, because this is exactly the mechanism
behind "falling water moves sideways" and it will matter the moment transport is
wired up.
